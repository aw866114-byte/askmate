WHAT TO HIRE FOR A BIG CLEAN-UP

For an Australian gardener facing a badly overgrown block, here is how the four options stack up.

Mini bobcat with a bucket

Good at: scraping out thick growth, pushing and lifting material, loading a trailer, moving dirt and rubbish fast.

Useless at: fine detail work, cutting close to fences and buildings, anything where a machine cannot fit.

Hire price: UNKNOWN

Tracked machine with a mulching head

Good at: chewing up woody scrub and small trees into mulch on the spot.

Useless at: grass, dirt and rubbish — a mulcher has to be fed by hand, and it cannot eat soil, rocks or junk.

Hire price: UNKNOWN

Petrol brushcutter with a circular blade

Good at: cutting down thick grass, blackberry and light scrub by hand, getting into tight spots.

Useless at: moving anything — it cuts but does not collect, so every bit still has to be raked and loaded by hand.

Hire price: UNKNOWN

Hand tools only

Good at: nothing on a big block — fine for a small garden, hopeless for a clean-up.

Useless at: volume. Days of work, and the trailer still has to be loaded by hand.

Hire price: UNKNOWN

For a badly overgrown block that must be scraped out and loaded onto a trailer, the bobcat wins. A mulcher has to be fed by hand, and half the material is grass, dirt and rubbish — which a mulching head cannot take. The bobcat scrapes it all up, pushes it into a pile, and loads the trailer itself. That is the machine that turns a week of hand work into a day.
