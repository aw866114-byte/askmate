WHEN TO GO FISHING

Port Macquarie - the Hastings River, the breakwall, Town Beach, Lighthouse Beach and Settlement Point. Best months, best part of the tide, best time of day, and the local spots. Anything not confirmed is marked UNKNOWN.

Dusky Flathead

Best months: late spring to summer (November to February).

Best tide: run-out, first few hours of the outgoing.

Best time of day: first light and late afternoon into dusk.

Where: sandy gutters and drop-offs in the Hastings River, sand flats off Settlement Point, sand edges off Town Beach. Cast along weed lines.

Bream

Best months: all year, picks up autumn to winter.

Best tide: the first couple of hours of either run, plus the change of water.

Best time of day: dawn and dusk, and at night in summer.

Where: the breakwall rocks, snags and structure along the Hastings River, stone walls near Settlement Point.

Whiting

Best months: summer to early autumn.

Best tide: the run-in, as water pushes onto sand banks and gutters.

Best time of day: early morning, soft light.

Where: Town Beach gutters, the sand flats at the river mouth, Lighthouse Beach surf gutters on calm days.

Mulloway (Jewfish)

Best months: autumn to winter; June to August is prime.

Best tide: night, especially the dark hours around the big run of a full or new moon.

Best time of day: night into dawn.

Where: the breakwall, deep holes in the Hastings River, and the river entrance channel.

Tailor

Best months: spring, and again in autumn.

Best tide: run-in, when white water moves over the sand.

Best time of day: dawn and dusk; they also feed at night.

Where: the breakwall, the surf gutters off Town Beach and Lighthouse Beach, the river mouth.

Luderick (Blackfish)

Best months: winter, June to August - the classic season.

Best tide: run-in, when wash covers the weed and rock.

Best time of day: morning and again late afternoon; can fish all daylight.

Where: the rock walls and Breakwall with green weed growing on them, weed beds in the river near Settlement Point.

Mangrove Jack

Best months: summer, December to March.

Best tide: run-in high, pushing water over snags and mangroves.

Best time of day: change of light, dawn and dusk.

Where: structure up the Hastings and surrounding creeks. NOTE: mangrove jack stakes are uncommon this far south of their main range - how regularly they turn up here is UNKNOWN.

Snapper

Best months: winter, June to August.

Best tide: dawn and dusk on a moving run, or night.

Best time of day: low light, night into first light.

Where: the offshore reefs - not a shore fish in Port. How well the estuarine spots above fish for snapper anyway is UNKNOWN; the breakwall at night is the shore bet.

Run-in Versus Run-out Tide

Run-in means the water is coming in - rising, moving up the river and onto the beach gutters. Run-out means it is going back out - falling, draining the river and the flats.

Fish feed on moving water. A running tide pushes bait, crabs, worms and bits of food past where the fish are holding, and it carries the fish's smell so they can hunt with their nose and their feel, not just their eyes. On slack water - the top of the high or the bottom of the low - nothing moves, so the food is not coming past, and most species stop feeding.

The rule of thumb: get there about two hours before the change of tide and fish through the change. The last of the run is still pushing food, and the first of the new run starts it again. The strongest feeding spells are usually the first two to three hours after the run starts, especially early and late in the day.

Check the tide for the day at bom.gov.au/australia/tides.
