WHEN TO GO FISHING

Port Macquarie — the Hastings River, the breakwall, Town Beach, Lighthouse Beach and Settlement Point.

THE TWO YOU CAME FOR

Mulloway — the jewies

Months: autumn into winter. June to August is the pick.

Tide: the big runs. Around the dark of a new moon or the full, when the water really moves.

Time: night into first light. They are a night fish.

Where: the breakwall, the deep holes in the Hastings, and the entrance channel.

Remember: 70 cm and one fish. A 65 goes back.

Dusky flathead — the flatties

Months: late spring through summer, November to February.

Tide: the run-out, first couple of hours of the outgoing, as the flats drain.

Time: first light, and late afternoon into dusk.

Where: sandy gutters and drop-offs in the river, the sand flats off Settlement Point, sand edges off Town Beach. Work the weed lines.

Remember: the big girls over 70 go back. They are the breeders.

THE REST

Bream

All year, best autumn into winter. First couple of hours of either run, and right on the change. Dawn, dusk, and at night in summer. Breakwall rocks, snags and structure through the Hastings, the stone walls near Settlement Point.

Whiting

Summer into early autumn. The run-in, as water pushes up onto the banks. Early morning, soft light. Town Beach gutters, the sand flats at the river mouth, Lighthouse Beach gutters on a calm day.

Tailor

Spring, and again in autumn. The run-in, when white water rolls over the sand. Dawn and dusk. The beaches and the breakwall.

Luderick

Winter is the run. Best on a run-in over the wash. Weed or cabbage under a float. The breakwall and the rock walls.

Mangrove jack

Warm months only — summer. Around the top of the tide, in tight to snags and rock bars up the river. Dusk and after dark. They live in the timber and they go straight back into it.

Snapper

Cooler months off the reefs and the wash. Tide change either way. Dawn, dusk, and after dark.

RUN-IN OR RUN-OUT

Run-in is the tide coming in, water pushing up onto the flats and the beaches. It brings clean water and it lets fish get up onto ground that was dry an hour ago. Whiting and tailor like it.

Run-out is the tide draining back out. Everything that was hiding up on the flats gets washed down into the channels — prawns, crabs, small fish — and the predators sit at the bottom of the drain and wait. That is why flathead fish so well on the run-out.

Slack water is the hour or so at the top or the bottom when nothing is moving. It is usually the quietest fishing of the day.

The rule underneath all of it: fish feed on moving water. Moving water carries food and it makes bait fish lose their footing. Pick your session around the run, not around the clock.
